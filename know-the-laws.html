01Feeding rights6 questions

Q1. Can my RWA stop me from feeding stray dogs in our society?+

No, your RWA cannot stop you - feeding community dogs is a legally protected activity, and any RWA prohibition is void.

Under Rule 8 of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, no RWA, housing society or private body can prohibit feeding of community dogs - this is an absolute prohibition. Rule 10 ABC Rules 2023 declares feeding of community animals a protected and lawful activity. Article 21 read with Article 51A(g) of the Constitution protects both your right to feed and the dogs' right to live in their territory. RWAs are subject to constitutional scrutiny under Article 226 because they exercise power over fundamental rights.

The Supreme Court in AWBI v Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547 held that animals have a fundamental right to life and dignity under Article 21. The Delhi High Court in Dr Maya D Chablani v Smt Radha Mittal CS(OS) 383/2016 (2021) declared all such RWA feeding bans void ab initio with no legal force whatsoever, and held that RWAs have no authority to create offences or impose fines on feeders. The Supreme Court judgment dated 19 May 2026 in In Re: City Hounded by Strays did not disturb these feeding protections - the institutional-area directions apply only to schools, hospitals, highways and similar premises, not residential societies.

  1. Continue feeding - send the RWA a written notice citing Rule 8 ABC Rules 2023 and the Chablani judgment, demanding withdrawal of the ban within 48 hours.
  2. Request the RWA to designate a feeding spot within the society compound under Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023 - this is the lawful resolution.
  3. If any guard or RWA member physically obstructs you, file an FIR under BNS 2023 Section 351 (criminal intimidation) and Section 221 (obstructing public function) at the local police station.
  4. If police refuse the FIR, approach the Judicial Magistrate under BNSS 2023 Section 175(3) citing Lalita Kumari v State of UP (2014) 2 SCC 1.
Q2. The security guard is blocking me from feeding street dogs. What can I do?+

Under Rule 10 of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, feeding community animals is a protected and lawful activity. Rule 8 ABC Rules 2023 prohibits any RWA, housing society or private body - including their employed staff - from preventing feeding. Article 51A(g) of the Constitution makes compassion for living creatures a fundamental duty of every citizen, and Article 21 protects this right. Under BNS 2023 Section 351, criminal intimidation by a person against you exercising a lawful right is a non-bailable offence. Under BNS Section 221, obstructing a person performing a lawful act is punishable.

The Delhi High Court in Dr Maya D Chablani v Smt Radha Mittal CS(OS) 383/2016 (2021) held that RWAs and their guards cannot prevent feeding and any such interference is unlawful. The Delhi High Court in People for Animals v MD Mohazzim (2015) held that FIRs and complaints against animal feeders are an abuse of legal process. The Supreme Court in AWBI v Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547 recognised animals' right to dignity under Article 21.

  1. Record the obstruction on video immediately - this is admissible as electronic evidence under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023.
  2. File an FIR at the local police station under BNS 2023 Section 351 and Section 221, naming the guard and the RWA that employs him.
  3. Send the RWA a written notice citing Rule 8 ABC Rules 2023 and the Chablani judgment, holding them vicariously liable for their guard's actions and demanding written assurance of non-interference within 48 hours.
  4. If police refuse the FIR, approach the Judicial Magistrate under BNSS 2023 Section 175(3) citing Lalita Kumari v State of UP (2014) 2 SCC 1.

Rule 10 ABC Rules 2023 makes feeding community animals a protected lawful activity. Q4. The RWA has fined me for feeding stray dogs. Is this legal?+

No, the fine is illegal and unenforceable - the RWA has no legal authority to penalise you for feeding community animals.

Under Rule 8 of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, RWAs and housing societies CANNOT prohibit feeding of community dogs - any rule or resolution to the contrary is void.   2. If the RWA attempts to deduct the fine from maintenance, threatens to cut water or electricity, or denies common facilities, file an FIR under BNS 2023 Section 308 (extortion) and Section 351 (criminal intimidation).

Q5. Where am I legally allowed to feed community dogs in my colony?+

You are legally allowed to feed community dogs anywhere within your colony where it does not cause demonstrable obstruction - and your RWA is legally required to designate a feeding spot if you request one.

Under Rule 10 of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, feeding of community animals is a protected and lawful activity. Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023 requires local authorities and RWAs to designate feeding spots within every municipal ward and residential area - this duty was reinforced by the Supreme Court. Rule 8 ABC Rules 2023 prohibits RWAs from blocking feeding altogether. Article 21 read with Article 51A(g) of the Constitution gives feeders constitutional standing.

The Supreme Court in In Re: City Hounded by Strays 2025 INSC 1018 (22 August 2025) directed that designated feeding spaces be made available in every municipal ward. The Supreme Court judgment dated 19 May 2026 reaffirmed the ABC Rules 2023 framework for residential areas - feeding rights remain fully intact. The Delhi High Court in Dr Maya D Chablani v Smt Radha Mittal CS(OS) 383/2016 (2021) directed RWAs to designate feeding spots within their compounds, not exclude feeders entirely.

  1. Send a written request to your RWA and the local municipal officer to designate a feeding spot within the colony under Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023, suggesting a low-traffic location such as a corner of the parking area or near the boundary wall.
  2. Until a spot is designated, feed at a location that does not block roads, building entrances, or children's play areas - this avoids legitimate complaints while exercising your right.
  3. Keep the feeding area clean - dispose of leftovers and bowls within 30 minutes - this defeats any nuisance counter-complaint.
  4. If the RWA refuses to designate a spot within 30 days, file a complaint with your municipal corporation citing Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023 and the 22 August 2025 Supreme Court direction.
Q6. The RWA has designated only one feeding spot for 30 community dogs in our colony. Is one spot enough or can we demand more?+

One spot for 30 dogs is not legal compliance - it is de facto prohibition of feeding, and you can demand multiple adequate spots proportional to the dog population.

Under Rule 8 of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, RWAs CANNOT prohibit feeding of community dogs - and a single spot that cannot reasonably accommodate the dog population in question is constructive prohibition, not compliance. Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023 requires designation of feeding spots that are functional and accessible - not nominal. Rule 10 ABC Rules 2023 protects feeding as a lawful activity. Article 14 of the Constitution prohibits arbitrary administrative action - designating one spot for 30 dogs when multiple are reasonably needed is arbitrary. Article 21 read with Article 51A(g) protects both the feeder's right and the dogs' right to dignified access to food. Designated spots must allow dogs to feed without conflict among themselves - a welfare standard under PCA Act 1960 Section 11(1)(h).

The Delhi High Court in Dr Maya D Chablani v Smt Radha Mittal CS(OS) 383/2016 (2021) held that designation of feeding spots must be reasonable and must actually permit feeding, not obstruct it - a token spot is no spot. The Supreme Court in In Re: City Hounded by Strays 2025 INSC 1018 (22 August 2025) directed that designated feeding spaces be made available in every ward, plural and adequate to local conditions. The Supreme Court in AWBI v Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547 anchored animal welfare in Article 21 - which includes adequate food access.

  1. Send the RWA a written demand citing Rule 20 ABC Rules 2023 and the Chablani judgment - state the dog count, the inadequacy of one spot (overcrowding causes inter-dog aggression, missed feeders, food competition), and demand reasonably distributed feeding spots across the colony in proportion to the dog population.
  2. Propose specific additional locations - low-traffic corners, edges of common areas, near service gates - and offer to coordinate feeding times to keep each spot clean within 30 minutes.
  3. If the RWA refuses, file a complaint with the State Animal Welfare Board, the Municipal Corporation under Rule 20, and the Registrar of Cooperative Societies citing arbitrary administrative action under Article 14.
  4. File a writ petition under Article 226 before the High Court if the RWA remains rigid - Chablani is direct authority that token designation is not compliance, and Article 14 makes arbitrary inadequacy challengeable.

Q7. The municipality is catching stray dogs from my area and not returning them. What can I do?+

The municipality is acting unlawfully - sterilised community dogs from residential areas must be returned to the same locality, and you can demand their return immediately.

Under Rule 11(19) of the Animal Birth Control Rules 2023, treated community dogs must be released back to the original locality from where they were picked up - only rabid or proven dangerous dogs may be permanently sheltered. Rule 7 ABC Rules 2023 explicitly prohibits relocation of community dogs from residential areas. Rule 3 ABC Rules 2023 places a mandatory, non-derogable duty on every urban local body to follow the ABC framework. Article 21 read with Article 51A(g) of the Constitution protects the dogs' right to their territory.

The Supreme Court in AWBI v People for Elimination of Stray Troubles SLP(C) 691/2009 (18 November 2015) held that ABC Rules have the force of law and that culling or permanent relocation of stray dogs is unlawful. The Supreme Court in In Re: City Hounded by Strays, judgment dated 19 May 2026, reaffirmed Rule 11(19) for residential areas - the shelter direction applies ONLY to institutional premises like schools, hospitals and highways, not residential colonies. The Supreme Court in AWBI v Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547 recognised animals' right to dignity under Article 21.

  1. Send a written demand to the Municipal Commissioner and the ward officer citing Rule 11(19) and Rule 7 ABC Rules 2023, demanding return of the dogs to your locality within 7 days - mention each dog's identification mark or photograph if available.
  2. Send a copy to the State Animal Welfare Board demanding inspection of the catching operation and the holding facility under PCA Act 1960 Section 11.
  3. File an FIR under BNS 2023 Section 303 (theft) and PCA Act Section 11 if the dogs were beaten or injured during capture.
  4. If the municipality does not respond within 7 days, file a writ petition under Article 226 before the High Court - cite the 19 May 2026 judgment which mandates ABC framework compliance for residential areas.

The five freedoms under AWBI v Nagaraja include freedom to express natural behaviour - dogs naturally rove within and beyond their core territory. The Supreme Court in AWBI v Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547 anchored the five freedoms in Article 21 - including freedom of natural behaviour and movement.
